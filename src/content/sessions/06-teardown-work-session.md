No new material this week. The set is work time, structured around peer
critique circles rather than a demo, because a teardown's weaknesses — a
vague tell, an unfollowed money trail, an unnamed audience — surface faster
under a stranger's questions than under your own re-reading.

## Bring

A draft of your Slop Teardown, however rough, and the artefact it's about
(linked, or precisely described if invented). Half a page of notes is enough
to work from; a blank page is not.

## In the session

**Critique circles (35 min).** Groups of three or four. Each student gets
five minutes to present their artefact and draft tell-inventory, then five
minutes of group feedback checked against one fixed list: does the draft name
tells with a timestamp or specific detail, rather than a general impression
(week 2)? Does it follow the money — what the artefact cost this way, what it
would have cost the traditional way, who's better off (week 4)? Does it name
the artefact's actual intended audience and the effect the artefact is built
to produce on them, rather than just describing what it depicts (week 5)?
Does it read as an argument with a position, rather than an inventory of
observations? A group that can't answer one of these about a draft has found
the draft's weakest section; say so plainly.

**Revision time (remainder).** Work alone or in your group on whatever the
critique surfaced. Idris is circulating for the specific, mechanical
questions — "is this actually a timestamp-anchored tell or a vibe" — not for
opinions on your artefact choice.

## Afterwards

Submit the Slop Teardown. There is no set exercise to carry forward from
this week the way other weeks carry a shot list or a worksheet forward — the
deliberate point of week 6 is that the only thing produced this week is the
argument itself.
