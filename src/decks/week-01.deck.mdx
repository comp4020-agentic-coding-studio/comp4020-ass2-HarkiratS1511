# Taxonomy of Slop

SLOP2758, week 1

---

## The claim

AI-generated video is not badly-made film. It is a genre, with its own
conventions, its own economics, and its own audience that has stopped
noticing the tells.

Film school teaches melodrama as a genre. This course teaches slop the same
way — seriously, historically, without apologising for the subject.

---

## What "slop" is not

- not "any AI-generated video"
- not a synonym for "bad"
- not a phase we're all waiting to age out of

A well-executed slop artefact still has six fingers somewhere. That's not a
production error. It's the genre's signature, the way a swelling string
section is melodrama's.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## A genre is defined by its tells, not its intentions

---

## Tell #1 — The Uncanny Hand

Six fingers. Four fingers. A thumb where a pinky should be. The model has
never seen a hand end an argument it started.

Fixable with inpainting. Almost nobody bothers, because the audience for this
content was never looking at the hands.

---

## Tell #2 — The Morphing Logo

A brand's logo that reads correctly for one frame in three. Letters that
almost spell the product name. The AI has a stronger prior on "logo-shaped"
than on any specific logo, and it shows.

---

## Tell #3 — The Affectless Narrator

A voiceover with correct prosody and no stakes. Every sentence lands with the
same weight, because the model isn't performing meaning — it's performing the
average of every video essay it was trained on.

---

## Tell #4 — Physics That Almost Holds

Cloth that drapes correctly until it doesn't. Water that pools uphill for one
second. Gravity as a suggestion the model is broadly in favour of.

---

## Tell #5 — The Infinite Zoom Into Nothing

A camera push toward a detail that resolves into more texture, never an
object. The model doesn't know what's supposed to be there, so it keeps
generating "closer."

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## "A genre defined by its errors is still a genre"

If melodrama's tell is the swelling string, slop's tell is the sixth finger.
Both are load-bearing. Neither is a flaw the form is trying to hide.

---

## Why a whole semester

Because the tells above are already dated. New models fix old tells and grow
new ones. A course that just lists 2026's failure modes expires on schedule —
one that teaches how to read a generative model's priors keeps working on
next year's model too.

---

## How the weeks work

- **Lecture** — theory, history, case study: what a tell tells you about the
  model that made it
- **Studio** — hands-on production: build the thing, don't just diagnose it

Same content, two registers. You'll do both every week.

---

## Where this goes

- wk 6 — **Slop Teardown**: take a real artefact apart, tell by tell
- wk 9 — **Slop Pipeline**: automate one archetype at scale, and cost it out
- wk 12 — **Direct a Slop Feature**: commit to a genre and hold it for the
  full runtime

Analysis is one assessment. Making it is the other two. That ratio is the
course's actual thesis.
