## The brief

> Commit to one slop feature — one story, one visual language, one genre —
> and hold that commitment for its full runtime.

This is the assessment the rest of the semester has been rehearsing for, and
it is not scored on runtime or on any single shot. It is scored on whether you
can hold a commitment across more than one piece of footage without it
drifting. One impressive clip does not satisfy this brief. A trailer with
sharp visual language followed by promotional cuts that quietly abandon it —
different narrator cadence, different colour grade, a tell that vanishes
halfway through — fails the actual test, whatever any individual clip looks
like in isolation.

Choose a genre and a slop aesthetic deliberately, not by accepting whatever a
model handed you on the first render: decide which tells belong to your
feature (a specific voiceover cadence, a specific way faces or logos
misbehave, a specific too-smooth camera move) and apply them on purpose,
consistently, because a genre is defined by what it does reliably, not by
what one lucky render did once. Sustained coherence is the hard part
precisely because it is invisible when it works and obvious the moment it
slips.

## What you submit

One of:

- a single continuous narrative piece, 3–5 minutes, telling one story start
  to finish, or
- a trailer (60–90 seconds) plus at least two linked promotional cuts (a
  character piece, a behind-the-scenes bit, a teaser — your call) that all
  visibly belong to the same feature

Either way, submit a director's statement (500–800 words) alongside the work:
name the one story, the one visual language, and the one genre you chose, and
say which decision you were most tempted to break and why you didn't (or, if
you did break it somewhere, say where and whether that was a mistake or a
deliberate exception).

The `marking:` block above weights sustained coherence above single-shot
craft — a rough feature that holds its commitment for the whole runtime beats
a polished one that only holds it for the trailer.
