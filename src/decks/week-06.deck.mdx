# Four Lenses, One Argument

SLOP2758, week 6

---

## The claim

Five weeks in, you don't have one skill called "analysis." You have four
distinct tools, built in a specific order, for a reason.

A teardown that only uses one of them reads as a list. A teardown that runs
all four reads as an argument.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## None of the four is optional

---

## Lens 1 — Tells (week 2)

"It looks fake" is not evidence. A frame number is.

Naming the exact second a hand gains a finger, or the exact clause where a
voiceover's cadence goes too smooth to be a read take, is the teardown's
evidentiary floor. Skip it, and the economics or genre reading has nothing to
stand on.

---

## Lens 2 — Direction (week 3)

Prompting-as-direction reads backward from a finished artefact to the choices
— or missing choices — that produced it.

Not "the continuity is bad." *Which* directorial decision — an unspecified
wardrobe detail, an uncontrolled camera move — the continuity failure traces
back to, and what that implies about how much direction the artefact got
before it shipped.

---

## Lens 3 — Economics (week 4)

Not "this was probably cheap to make." A cost-per-output figure, set against
what the traditional version would have cost, with a name for who pockets the
difference.

This is the lens most drafts underuse, because it means guessing at numbers
nobody will confirm. Guess anyway, and state the assumption. An account that
admits its numbers are estimates beats one that avoids numbers altogether.

---

## Lens 4 — Genre (week 5)

Name what's being pastiched, and by whom.

An artefact's genre conventions tell you what it's borrowing and what
audience-facing promise it's making — comfort-on-demand, credibility without
a falsifiable claim, urgency about history that was never checked. Naming the
sub-genre correctly has usually already answered who this is for.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## "Each lens describes a different part of the same object"

What it does. How it was made. Why it was worth making that way. What it's
pretending to be.

---

## Why all four, together

Tells tell you *what it does*. Direction tells you *how it was made*.
Economics tells you *why it was worth making that way*. Genre tells you
*what it's pretending to be*.

Run all four against one artefact and you're arguing about the object as a
whole — the difference between analysis and inventory.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Before this session

Bring a draft Slop Teardown, however rough, and the artefact it's about.

The set hour is critique circles, checked against one list: timestamped
tells, a followed money trail, a named audience, an argument rather than an
inventory. No new production exercise this week — the only thing due is the
argument itself.
