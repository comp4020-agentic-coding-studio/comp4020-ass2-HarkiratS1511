## Before the session

Bring your capstone story bible and whatever you've produced against it so
far, even if it's two test clips and a title.

## In the session

Draft a director's statement, 300–500 words, for the piece you're making.
Structure it the way the genre actually expects — not because a personal
essay is naturally shaped this way, but because the director's statement is a
genre convention with its own moves, borrowed wholesale from festival
programme notes and pressroom copy: a paragraph of intent (why this story,
why now), a paragraph on process (what was actually made with what tools,
stated plainly rather than mystified), and a paragraph that addresses the
piece's ethical choices head-on — whose likeness, voice or labour the piece
depends on, and why you think that's defensible or how you've limited it.

Then draft the harder sentence: a one-line synthetic-content disclosure, as
if the piece were being submitted to a festival that requires one. Say what
was AI-generated and what wasn't, in language specific enough that a
programmer reading a hundred of these could actually tell your piece apart
from the next one's disclosure. "AI tools were used in the making of this
film" discloses nothing; "the lead performance is a synthetic likeness
generated from a licensed voice model; all backgrounds are camera-original"
discloses something.

## Afterwards

Bring both to week 12. The capstone work session assumes the statement
exists in draft, because the last week of studio time is for finishing the
piece the statement describes, not for starting to think about what it
means.
