## The brief

> Take one slop artefact apart in public and show your work.

Every other assessment in this course asks you to make something that commits
to its own fakeness. This one asks you to do the opposite: find something that
is already trying to pass, and explain exactly how it fails, for whom, and at
what price. That is the point of keeping one analysis piece in a
production-heavy course — a director who cannot diagnose a six-fingered hand,
a morphing logo, or an affectless narrator in someone else's footage is
directing blind in their own. Do not let this drift into a making exercise:
no clips, no remixes, no "here is my version" — the deliverable is the
argument, not a production.

Pick one artefact: a real ad, explainer, or product demo you suspect (or can
show) is synthetic, or a specific invented one described in enough detail that
a reader could picture the exact seconds you mean. Vague genre gestures —
"AI videos often look uncanny" — do not count as an artefact. One artefact,
named or precisely constructed, taken apart in detail.

A strong teardown does three things a plausible-sounding one skips. It locates
the tells instead of gesturing at them: not "the movement looks off" but the
specific frame where a hand gains a finger, or the second where the voiceover's
cadence goes too smooth to be a read take. It follows the money: what this
artefact replaced, what it cost to make this way instead, and who is better
off for the substitution. And it identifies who the artefact is actually
for — a product demo built to be skimmed past, not scrutinised, is aimed at an
audience with a specific tolerance for wrongness, and naming that tolerance is
half the argument.

## What you submit

A written teardown, 1000–1500 words, structured however best serves the
argument (a running commentary keyed to timestamps works; so does a
thesis-led essay with evidence folded in). If the artefact is publicly
available, link it and cite the specific timestamps your argument depends on.
If it is invented, describe it precisely enough at the top of the piece that
the rest of the teardown reads as an analysis of a fixed thing, not a moving
target.

The `marking:` block above is weighted: it rewards specificity of evidence and
the strength of the economic argument over polish of prose.
