Last studio of the semester, and it's work time, not new material: the same
three-check-in structure as week 9, timed around finishing rather than
assembling. Bring the piece in whatever state it's actually in, the story
bible it's supposed to obey, and the director's statement draft from week 11.

## What the check-ins are for

The check-ins exist to catch the specific way a capstone fails at this stage:
not "the individual clips look bad" but "the individual clips no longer agree
with each other," which is the sustained-coherence problem from week 10
showing up under deadline pressure, when it's tempting to ship a shot that
breaks the bible's colour rule because re-rendering it would blow the
timeline. Say so out loud in a check-in if it's happening; a story bible you
knowingly broke and can explain is a stronger submission than one you quietly
hoped nobody would check against.

Update the director's statement to match whatever the piece became, not what
you planned in week 11 — a statement that describes a cut you abandoned is
worse than no statement, because it's the first thing a marker checks the
piece against.

## Afterwards

Submit Direct a Slop Feature. There is no week 13.
