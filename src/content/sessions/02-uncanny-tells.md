## Before the session

Nothing to bring — the specimens are already on the shelf from week 1. Read
the lecture first; you're using its vocabulary today, not inventing your own.

## In the session

**Round one — swap and log (30 min).** Pair up and trade specimens. For the
clip you didn't bring, produce a tell-log: a numbered list of timestamps, each
naming one specific tell (uncanny hand, morphing logo, temporal flicker, the
affectless narrator, the too-smooth cadence, or a tell the lecture didn't name
that you can still describe precisely). "The face looks weird at 0:14" is not
an entry. "0:14 — the left eyebrow's position resets between two nearly
identical frames" is.

**Round two — check the log, not the clip (15 min).** Return the log to
whoever brought the specimen. Their job is to verify each entry against the
actual frame, not to argue about whether it's a real tell — if the timestamp
doesn't show what the entry claims, the entry fails, regardless of whether the
clip is slop by some other measure.

**Round three — compare to week 1's working names (15 min).** Pull up the
informal stack-names from the specimen shelf. Where did the untrained sort
from week 1 land close to the formal tell categories, and where was it
wrong in an interesting way — a stack that grouped by subject matter
("cooking videos") rather than by production tell, say. Log the comparison
on the shelf next to the original entry.

## Afterwards

The tell-logs stay on the shelf. Week 6's Slop Teardown asks for exactly this
kind of timestamped, specific evidence, at greater length and against one
artefact you choose yourself — today is where the habit gets built, on
someone else's clip, where getting it wrong costs nothing.
