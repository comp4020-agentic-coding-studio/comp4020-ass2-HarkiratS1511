## Before the session

Read the screening and pick one sub-genre from it — the AI history explainer,
the product demo nobody filmed, or faith-based inspirational content — before
you arrive. Come with a rough idea of a specific fake product, historical
non-event, or devotional theme; you'll build it out in the room.

## In the session

**Round one — name the structure (15 min).** Before writing anything, list
the structural conventions of your chosen sub-genre as it actually appears on
the specimen shelf: the cold open hook, the tell-don't-show narration style,
the specific kind of resolution or call to action it ends on, whatever else
you've noticed across specimens. Three conventions, minimum, named plainly —
not "it feels inspirational," but "it ends on a direct-address blessing
delivered straight to camera."

**Round two — write the pastiche (35 min).** Draft a sixty-to-ninety-second
script or prompt sequence that commits fully to your chosen genre's
conventions — no irony, no wink to the audience, no hedging it as a parody.
The exercise is pastiche, not satire: the point is to hit the beats with the
same sincerity-agnostic precision the real specimens do, which is harder than
it sounds, because a script written *knowing* it's an exercise tends to leak
a joke the genre itself never tells.

**Round three — audit it (10 min).** Check your draft against the three
conventions you named in round one, convention by convention. Where it
doesn't land one, decide whether that's a gap in your draft or a sign the
convention you named wasn't actually load-bearing.

## Afterwards

Bring the script and the audit to week 6 — not to submit, but because
recognising a genre's conventions from the inside, by trying to reproduce
them, is exactly the skill the Slop Teardown asks you to run in reverse on
someone else's artefact.
