## Before the session

Have a rough idea, even a bad one, of what your Direct a Slop Feature piece
is about. You don't need a script; you need a genre and a look.

## In the session

One clip is easy. Five linked clips that read as one piece is the actual
difficulty of the capstone, and it's a coherence problem, not a generation
problem — the models can make any single frame; keeping the same frame's
rules true forty shots later is what breaks. Write a story bible: one to two
pages, plain prose or a table, stating a fixed colour or lighting rule (a LUT
description, a time-of-day constraint), a method for keeping a character or
setting consistent (a locked seed, a reference-image conditioning set, a
described costume and prop list if your tool doesn't support seed-locking),
and one recurring motif that has to appear in every piece (an object, a
camera move, a sound cue).

Then prove it: produce two test frames or clips from the bible, made minutes
apart or days apart, and check whether a stranger could tell they're from the
same piece without being told. If they can't, the bible is underspecified —
usually because "consistent lighting" was never turned into a rule concrete
enough to check, the same gap that lets a batch of b-roll drift from golden
hour to overcast within one reflection without anyone flagging it.

## Afterwards

The bible is a working document, not a deliverable — expect to revise it once
the capstone script exists. Bring it to week 11, where the director's
statement has to account for what the bible commits you to.
