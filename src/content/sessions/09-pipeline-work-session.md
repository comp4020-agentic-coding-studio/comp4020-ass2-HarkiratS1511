No new material this week — the studio is work time, timetabled around three
fifteen-minute check-in slots (early, midpoint, last-call) rather than a
single demo at the end, because a pipeline that looks finished at minute ten
of a check-in and breaks under questioning is exactly the failure mode the
teardown taught you to look for in other people's work.

## Bring

The week 7 pipeline diagram, the week 8 template with its slots filled five
ways, the cull rule and threshold, the scheduling plan, and a first pass at
the economics case: a cost-per-output figure (render minutes, API calls,
whatever your archetype actually costs) set against a plausible return (ad
revenue, lead generation, whatever your archetype is actually for). The
check-ins exist to catch a pipeline that's internally consistent but
economically fictional — a template producing outputs nobody would pay for,
at a cost nobody would recoup — before it's what gets submitted.

## Afterwards

Submit the Slop Pipeline. Week 10 assumes you now know what a system costs to
run, and turns to a different problem: keeping one running for longer than a
single batch without it drifting into something else.
