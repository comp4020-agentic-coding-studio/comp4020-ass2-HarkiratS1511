# Policies and support

## Late submissions

Work loses 5% of the available mark per day it is late, for up to five days.
After five days the submission is marked as a demonstration of timeliness
rather than content, and receives zero — this applies however good the render
looks. "The pipeline was still running" is not a circumstance, it is the
assessment; plan render time into your schedule the way you'd plan for a
camera that jams.

Extensions are granted for documented circumstances (medical, compassionate,
equipment failure with evidence) and are requested by emailing the convenor
**before** the deadline, not after. A request made once the deadline has
passed is a late submission with a longer story attached.

## Academic integrity, for a course about synthetic media

Most academic integrity policies exist to stop you from passing off a
machine's output as your own. This course inverts that: using generative
models is the medium, not the violation. What would be misconduct elsewhere —
disclosing that a diffusion model, an LLM, or an automated pipeline produced
the footage, the script, or the voiceover — is simply how the work gets made
here, and hiding it is not an option because the pipeline itself is often part
of what's being assessed.

Given that, integrity in SLOP2758 means something more specific than "declare
your AI use":

- **Declare your pipeline, not just that one existed.** Say which tools or
  models did which job, what you wrote or directed by hand, and what came out
  of the generator with no intervention. "AI-assisted" is not a pipeline
  description; "a diffusion model generated the base footage, I wrote and fed
  every prompt, and the voiceover is a text-to-speech pass over a script I
  wrote myself" is.
- **In the Slop Teardown, say plainly whether your artefact is found or
  invented.** Presenting an invented artefact as a real find is the one move
  that voids the assessment outright, because the teardown is an argument
  about something claimed to exist; the argument is only as good as that
  claim is honest.
- **Don't submit someone else's pipeline, prompt library, or teardown
  research as your own.** Building on a classmate's published prompt template
  is fine if you say so and change it into something that's actually yours;
  handing in their template unchanged is not different from handing in their
  essay unchanged.
- **Cite what a human wrote.** Direction, editing decisions, and the
  director's statement on the capstone are yours in the traditional sense —
  the usual rules about your own words apply there without modification.

If you are ever unsure whether a specific disclosure is enough, over-disclose
and ask; a marker who has to guess at your pipeline will mark the guess, not
the work.

## Getting help

Contact details and the best channel for each kind of question are on the
[people page](/people/) — email the convenor for anything that affects the
whole course (deadlines, marking, scope), and bring week-to-week build
questions to the Studio session rather than to an inbox. Studio staff read
questions asked in person faster than the same question in writing, because
most build problems are faster to show than to describe.

## Access and equity

If a disability, caring responsibility, or other circumstance affects your
ability to meet a deadline or attend a session, contact the convenor as early
as possible — ideally before it becomes urgent, not after. Adjustments are
made to the requirement, not to the standard the work is judged against.
